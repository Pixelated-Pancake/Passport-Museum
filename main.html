About

Imagine standing at a check in counter at the airport. Before letting you through, the clerk at the check in desk asks for your passport. Strength / Power Rankings

This is a subject of great debate, but basically, power rankings are just a fancy term used to descrbe which country imprisions their citizens the least has the least restrictions on which countries / the number of countries their citizens can visit.

These rankings are done by a lot of organisations, but there are some countries that consistently achieve top spots. It is to be noted that recently, the UAE snagged the #1 spot from Singapore, who held the title of the strongest passport for the last couple of years.

Other top contenders include Japan, South Korea, Luxembourg, Spain, Sweden, and Switzerland (thats's a lot of countries that start with S-)

Anatomy

Well, it's quite obvious that a passport is not an ordinary booklet. Fun fact: Australia's passport (the one you saw on the homepage) is the most expensive in the world, with an adult passport with a 10 year validity costing ~$275 💵

But what actually goes into a passport? Well, quite a lot of things, actually. And there are a fair share of features that are added for the sole purpose of preventing people from aquiring a plethora of fake aliases

The video below gives us a behind the scenes look into the making of a United States passport. While not all countries follow such a tight level of security, the US certainly likes to keep its passports in check.

As a TL;DR, passports are typically made of a front cover containing the issuing country's name, emblem, and a microchip. Next, the ID page contains all your cridentials, along with the MRZ - Machine Readable Zone. This is the part that machines scan to get your cridentials instantly.

There are also generally a lot of blank pages, intended for stamps when entering / exiting a country. However, some countries have switched to recordning entry and exit dates digitally for convinience

Border Control

Have you ever wondered what happens when you stand at that ever so long line for the guys to take a picture at you while they yell at you to remove your glasses? Well - consider your question answered (Even if you DIDNT ask for it, play along, okay??)

Behind the Scenes - The immigration system

It all begins as you hand over your passport to the (intimidating) immigration officer. He then opens up the first page of your passport and scans the MRZ - Machine Readable Zone. This is usually two lines of characters at the borrom of the identity page. The tiny microchip present in your passport is also scanned and verified to check for authenticity. Some minor checks such as passport validity, and visa verification (since visas are linked to passports) are checked.

And then, when you get your photo taken, verification checks occur, matching facial features, appearence, etc.

The cool part? Some countries have a cool feature where scanned passports are sent through various databases like INTERPOL's SLTD, or country - specific blacklists. But what happens if something doesnt match? If things like a mismatching photograph, a damaged passport, or an expired visa happen, the concerned traveller will most likely be referred for additional examination.

That's pretty vast for something that happens in a few minutes.
